The Exposure

The problem is not one AI system.
It is every system that AI touches.

AI operating in orbit, defense platforms, and high-reliability aerospace environments makes consequential decisions faster than any human reviewer can intervene. The governance frameworks protecting those environments were not built for that reality. Regulators, insurers, and licensing authorities are beginning to move. The window for proactive positioning is narrow.

Ground-in-the-loop oversight is structurally impossible at orbital scale

Signal round-trip times, contact windows, and node counts make real-time human review physically unavailable. An oversight architecture that requires ground contact to function is not an oversight architecture.

Existing audit infrastructure was not built for autonomous action

Traditional logging captures what happened. It cannot establish that a deterministic, human-authorized decision preceded each consequential AI action — a gap that is a material liability under emerging space, defense, and regulatory frameworks.

Documentation requirements are tightening across every relevant authority

EU AI Act Article 14, FCC and ITU constellation licensing, space insurance underwriting, and defense program office requirements are converging on the same demand: verifiable, continuous records of AI oversight activity. Organizations without that documentation face both regulatory and valuation risk.
